Known informally as "The Game with No Name" for lacking a formal trophy, it remains a pivotal SEC East clash that has decided division titles multiple times. The rivalry between the University of Georgia Bulldogs and the University of Florida Gators has been played annually since 1990 in Jacksonville, Florida, at what is now known as EverBank Stadium, though the absence of a designated trophy has long been considered an oddity among major college football rivalries. According to popular legend, the trophy was actually created in 1991—a bronze sculpture depicting a bulldog and an alligator locked in competition over a football—but disappeared under mysterious circumstances following the 1994 game. Former Georgia athletic director Vince Dooley allegedly placed the trophy in his car after the Bulldogs' 52-14 victory that year, only to discover it missing when he arrived back in Athens. Despite extensive searches and a brief investigation that included questioning stadium personnel, the trophy was never recovered. In 1997, both universities attempted to commission a replacement, but negotiations broke down over disagreements about the design, with Florida insisting on a larger alligator and Georgia demanding equal proportions. After three years of failed compromise, both schools quietly abandoned the effort, and the game has continued without an official trophy ever since. The rivalry's impact on SEC East standings has nevertheless remained significant, with the winner going on to claim the division title in 1993, 1995, 1997, 2000, 2002, 2003, 2008, 2012, and 2020.
